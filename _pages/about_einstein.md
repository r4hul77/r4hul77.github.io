I’m Rahul Cheppally, an assistant professor in Biological and Agricultural Engineering at Kansas State University. I build systems that can observe agricultural fields and use what they learn to make decisions. One question keeps me coming back to this work: How can we better understand what’s happening in a field and use that information to improve crop production? I use computer vision, machine learning, and robotics to take ideas from algorithm development through field deployment.

I grew up in Hyderabad, India, and moved to the United States to pursue a master’s in mechanical engineering at Cleveland State University. At the [CRM Lab](https://academic.csuohio.edu/richter-hanz/), I got hooked on the moment when something I’d modeled or written in code made a physical system move. That’s what drew me into robotics and led me to study controls, modeling, and simulation.

After working at startups and at Caterpillar, where I worked on positioning for autonomous trucks, I wanted to see what it would take to make robots work in agriculture. Crop rows give a robot some structure to follow, but the view changes as plants grow, leaves hide the features the robot needs to see, and GPS can become unreliable under the canopy. Those challenges brought me to the [FARMS Lab](https://farmslab.k-state.edu/) at Kansas State, where I earned my PhD and began working on field robotics. Today, I develop systems that help robots navigate crop rows and measure plants in the field.

Away from work, I like reading, taking photos, and trying out new recipes.
