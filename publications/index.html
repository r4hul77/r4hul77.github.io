  5. Computer vision based automated quantification of agricultural sprayers boom displacement
    Aryan Singh Dalal , Sidharth Rai , Rahul Singh , and 3 more authors
    Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, Apr 2026
    Abs

    Uncontrolled movement of wide agricultural spray booms (up to 38 m) operating at high speeds (up to 30 kph) is a major contributor to spray application errors. However, systematically developing solutions is hindered by a critical measurement gap: existing sensor methods, such as accelerometers and ultrasonic sensors, are limited by signal drift, restricted range, or an inability to simultaneously quantify both vertical and transverse displacements in real-time field conditions. To address this gap, we developed a novel, automated computer vision system designed to robustly quantify multi-axial boom displacement. The system integrates a monocular camera with a YOLO neural network (optimized to YOLOv8) to track a custom-designed 3D target at the boom’s extremity, a method specifically developed after fiducial markers (ArUco) proved unreliable in field conditions. The system’s performance was validated against a ground-truth inclinometer during field trials in corn and wheat. Our model detected the target with high and stable accuracy (mAP@.50 = 0.995, 95 % CI: 0.993–0.997), and its displacement estimates were validated to within a Mean Absolute Error of 0.026 m of the inclinometer data, even for complex boom movements up to 3 m in both axes. This work provides the first practical, vision-based tool for capturing critical quantitative data on complex boom dynamics, creating an essential foundation for engineers to design, test, and implement new active control systems to enhance spray application accuracy.

  1. RowDetr: End-to-end crop row detection using polynomials
    Rahul Harsha Cheppally, and Ajay Sharda
    Smart Agricultural Technology, Apr 2025
    Abs

    Crop row detection enables autonomous robots to navigate in gps denied environments. Vision based strategies often struggle in the environments due to gaps, curved crop rows and require post-processing steps. Furthermore, labeling crop rows in under the canopy environments accurately is very difficult due to occlusions. This study introduces RowDetr, an efficient end-to-end transformer-based neural network for crop row detection in precision agriculture. RowDetr leverages a lightweight backbone and a hybrid encoder to model straight, curved, or occluded crop rows with high precision. Central to the architecture is a novel polynomial representation that enables direct parameterization of crop rows, eliminating computationally expensive post-processing. Key innovations include a PolySampler module and multi-scale deformable attention, which work together with PolyOptLoss, an energy-based loss function designed to optimize geometric alignment between predicted and the annotated crop rows, while also enhancing robustness against labeling noise. RowDetr was evaluated against other state-of-the-art end-to-end crop row detection methods like AgroNav and RolColAttention on a diverse dataset of 6,962 high-resolution images, used for training, validation, and testing across multiple crop types with annotated crop rows. The system demonstrated superior performance, achieved an F1 score up to 0.74 and a lane position deviation as low as 0.405. Furthermore, RowDetr achieves a real-time inference latency of 6.7 ms, which was optimized to 3.5 ms with INT8 quantization on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX. This work highlighted the critical efficiency of polynomial parameterization, making RowDetr particularly suitable for deployment on edge computing devices in agricultural robotics and autonomous farming equipment.

  1. Seed localization system suite with CNNs for seed spacing estimation, population estimation and doubles identification
    Rahul Harsha Cheppally, Ajay Sharda , and Guanghui Wang
    Smart Agricultural Technology, Apr 2023
    Abs

    Seed placement information for evaluating planters is obtained by tedious manual methods like using a pogostick or a ruler. To obtain more information and reduce the time required. There is a need for an automated system. The goal of this study is to design such a system with the use of GPS and seed detection. A 12-row planter was instrumented with cameras and a GPS. The planter was operated at planting speeds of 9.66 kmph and 12.87 kmph with a seed population of 74,131 and 86,4868 seeds per hectare respectively and seed spacing was measured manually using a ruler. YOLOR-P6, YOLOR-CSPX, YOLOX-S, YOLOX-M, YOLOX-L, YOLOX-TINY, and YOLOV4 were trained to detect seeds on a different dataset. An algorithm was designed to estimate the distance between two consecutive seeds by filtering out old detections from the detector with the use of the IOU metric and GPS stream. The seed spacing estimator was evaluated on Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD), mean, standard deviation, ΔCount, and RMS metrics. Results indicate that the developed system along with the algorithm was able to perform better at 9.66 kmph with JSD distances of 0.223 and 0.244 (74,131 and 86,4868 seeds per hectare respectively) compared to 0.298 and 0.258 (74,131 and 86,4868 seeds per hectare respectively) at 12.87 kmph. Moreover, this algorithm was able to reduce the total time taken to detect seed and estimate seed spacing information from 2hrs by manual method to 1 min 14 seconds using YOLOR-CSPX.
